You can usually get a replacement car after a non-fault accident while your own car is off the road being repaired, or if it can't be fixed. Because another driver caused the accident, the cost of the replacement vehicle is claimed from their insurer, not from you. What you're entitled to depends on the circumstances, such as what you use your car for and how long it's off the road, so it's worth asking early.

This guide covers who pays, how a replacement car is arranged, what to check when you get one, and why it matters so much if you drive for work.

## Why a replacement car after a non-fault accident matters

Most of us rely on our car more than we notice until it's gone. The school run, the commute, the weekly shop, visiting family, getting to appointments: all of it becomes harder overnight. For some people it's more than inconvenient. If you drive a taxi, a private hire car or a delivery route, every day without a vehicle is a day without income.

The principle behind a non-fault claim is that you shouldn't be left out of pocket, or without transport, because of someone else's mistake. A replacement vehicle is part of putting things back as they were.

## Who pays for the replacement car?

In a non-fault claim, the cost of a replacement vehicle is claimed from the at-fault driver's insurer. You shouldn't need to pay for it yourself or claim it on your own policy.

A few things can affect what's provided:

- **Why you need a car.** Getting to work, family needs and driving for a living all matter.
- **The kind of car you drive.** The aim is a replacement that does the same job as yours.
- **How long yours is off the road.** That depends on the repairs, or on whether the car can be repaired at all.
- **Whether fault is clear.** Where it's disputed, the details and evidence matter more.

Because each accident is different, nobody can honestly promise exactly what you'll get before they've heard what happened. A good first conversation should explain it plainly.

## How a replacement car is arranged

The order of events is usually straightforward.

1. **You report the accident** and explain that it wasn't your fault.
2. **Your situation is checked**: what happened, what you use your car for, and whether it's drivable.
3. **Recovery is arranged** if your car can't be driven, taking it from the roadside to somewhere safe.
4. **A replacement car is arranged** so you're not left without transport while yours is assessed and repaired.
5. **Repairs go ahead**, and the replacement stays with you while your own car is off the road.
6. **You hand the replacement back** when your own car is returned, or when the claim for a car that can't be repaired is settled.

## What to check when you get your replacement car

When the replacement arrives, take a few minutes before you drive off:

- **Walk round it and photograph any existing marks**, so there's no question about them later.
- **Check who is covered to drive it**, especially if other people in your household drive your car.
- **Ask what to do if something goes wrong** with it, and keep that number handy.
- **Ask about fuel and mileage**, so you know what's expected when you hand it back.
- **Keep the paperwork** in your claim folder with everything else from the accident.

Treat it as you would your own car, and don't use it for anything your own car isn't used for without asking first.

## What if your car can't be repaired?

Sometimes the damage is too serious, or would cost more to fix than the car is worth, and the car is treated as a write-off. That's a stressful thing to hear, especially if you've had the car for years or rely on it every day.

If that happens in a non-fault claim, you shouldn't be left without transport while it's sorted out. The replacement car can stay with you while the claim for your own car is settled, so you have time to work out what to do next rather than rushing into a decision. Ask how it works in your case, and keep any paperwork about your car's condition, mileage and recent work, because it helps show what the car was like before the accident.

If you're unsure about anything you're told about your car, ask for it in writing and take your time before you agree to it.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Paying for a hire car yourself and hoping to claim it back later.** Ask first; it may already be part of a non-fault claim.
- **Claiming on your own policy without asking about alternatives.** That can mean paying an excess and waiting for it to come back.
- **Not mentioning that you drive for work.** It can change what you need, so say so on the first call.
- **Waiting to see how long repairs take before asking.** The sooner you ask, the sooner a replacement can be arranged.
- **Skipping the walk-round.** Photograph the replacement car before you drive it.

In a non-fault claim, the cost of the replacement vehicle is claimed from the at-fault driver's insurer, not from you. Eligibility depends on the circumstances of the accident.

### Can I get a replacement car if mine can still be driven?

It depends on the damage and on whether the car is safe and legal to drive. If it needs repairs, a replacement can cover the time it's off the road.
