A non-fault accident claim is how you recover the costs of an accident that someone else caused. Costs such as recovering your vehicle, a replacement car or bike while yours is off the road, and repairs are claimed from the at-fault driver's insurer rather than from you or your own policy. If you were hurt, an injury claim can run alongside it. Whether you can claim, and what for, depends on the circumstances of the accident.

This guide explains what counts as non-fault, who pays for what, how the claim runs from the first call to the end, and the questions people usually ask along the way.

## What counts as a non-fault accident?

Generally, an accident is non-fault when another road user caused it and you did nothing to contribute. Some common examples:

- you were hit from behind while stopped or slowing in traffic
- you were hit while your car was parked
- a driver pulled out of a side road or junction into your path
- a vehicle changed lanes into you
- you were a passenger in any vehicle

Not every accident is clear cut. Sometimes both drivers share some of the blame, or the evidence takes time to piece together. If you're not sure, that's normal. It's worth talking it through with someone who deals with these claims before you assume anything either way.

## How a non-fault accident claim works, step by step

The details vary from claim to claim, but the shape is usually the same.

### 1. You report what happened

You give the facts: when and where, what happened, the other driver's details and registration, any photos and witness details, and your own vehicle and insurance details. Don't worry if you don't have everything.

### 2. The claim is checked

Someone looks at whether the accident appears to be non-fault and what you may be entitled to. This is the point to ask questions.

### 3. You're kept moving

If your vehicle can't be driven, it can be recovered from the roadside to somewhere safe. If it's off the road, a replacement car or motorbike can be arranged so you're not left without transport.

### 4. Repairs are arranged

Your vehicle is assessed and repaired. In a non-fault claim, the cost of the repairs is claimed from the at-fault driver's insurer.

### 5. The claim is settled

The at-fault driver's insurer is dealt with until the claim is settled. If you were hurt, any injury claim is supported until it's settled too.

## Who pays in a non-fault accident claim?

This is the question most people ask first, and the answer is the reason non-fault claims exist. When another driver caused the accident, the costs of putting things right are claimed from their insurer. That can include:

- recovering your vehicle from the roadside
- a replacement vehicle while yours is off the road
- the repairs to your vehicle
- other losses caused by the accident, depending on the circumstances

## Your own insurer or a claims management company?

After a non-fault accident you'll usually tell your own insurer, because most policies require it. But you don't have to claim through your own policy if the accident wasn't your fault.

Claiming on your own policy can mean paying your excess first and waiting to get it back, and the claim may appear on your record even though you weren't to blame. Going through the at-fault side instead keeps the costs where they belong.

An accident claim management company deals with the at-fault insurer and the paperwork on your behalf. ## What makes a non-fault accident claim go smoothly

Good evidence and quick action make the biggest difference.

- **Details from the scene.** The other driver's name, address, phone number, registration and insurer. See our guide on [what to do after a car accident](/guides/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident) for the full list.
- **Photos.** The damage to every vehicle, the road layout and the scene, taken as soon as it's safe.
- **Witnesses.** Names and phone numbers of anyone who saw it happen.
- **A record of costs and calls.** Keep receipts and note every phone call with the date and who you spoke to.
- **Starting early.** The sooner the claim begins, the sooner recovery, a replacement vehicle and repairs can be arranged.

What tends to slow things down is missing details, a disagreement about fault with no evidence either way, or letting the claim drift while you try to sort it out alone.

## Keeping track while your claim runs

A claim can involve several people: the at-fault insurer, a garage, a recovery firm and whoever supplies your replacement vehicle. Keep a simple log so nothing gets lost.

- the date and time of every call, who you spoke to and what was agreed
- copies of every letter and email, in one folder
- receipts for any costs the accident caused you
- notes on any injury: when it started, how it affects your day, and any appointments

## Frequently asked questions

### Will a non-fault accident claim cost me anything?

For a non-fault accident, costs such as recovery, a replacement vehicle and repairs are claimed from the at-fault driver's insurer, not from you. Injury claims are no win, no fee, and any fee on a successful claim is explained before you go ahead.

### What if I'm not sure the accident was my fault?

Call and talk it through. Whether an accident is non-fault depends on the details, and it's better to ask than to guess. There's no obligation.

### Can I claim if I was a passenger?

Passengers are very rarely at fault, so if you were hurt you may be able to make an injury claim. Call us and we'll explain your options.

### Do I need to tell my own insurer?

Most policies require you to tell your insurer about any accident. Telling them isn't the same as claiming on your own policy.
